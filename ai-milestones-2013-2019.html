Overview

I contributed content covering AI developments from 2013 through 2019 to a group timeline presentation. My section covers changes in language processing, generative models, reinforcement learning, and deep neural networks.

The project

The contribution appears on two slides: “AI Development: 2013–2015” and “AI Development: 2017–2019.” Each pairs dated milestones with short explanations of the development and its significance.

My contribution

I contributed the content for the presentation’s 2013–2019 section, shown on slides 8 and 9. The complete timeline is a group project.

The goal

Explain a period of AI development in a clear chronological format, connecting important models and tools with the capabilities they introduced. This section contributes to the group’s broader historical timeline.

My approach

  1. Cover the earlier developments. The 2013–2015 slide introduces word2vec, GANs, attention in machine translation, DeepMind’s Atari work, TensorFlow, ResNet, and OpenAI’s announcement.
  2. Continue through 2019. The next slide covers Transformers, AlphaZero, the original GPT, BERT, and GPT-2.
  3. Keep the explanations brief. The content describes each event in accessible terms, including relationships between words, learning through rewards, self-play, and text generation.

Tools & technologies

  • Presentation format: the group deliverable is a PowerPoint (.pptx) file.
  • Technical subjects: language models, generative models, attention, reinforcement learning, and deep networks.
  • Supporting references: papers and project announcements listed in the presentation.

Why it matters

What makes it useful

The section turns a sequence of technical milestones into short explanations that readers can scan alongside dates. It provides context for how language processing and other AI capabilities developed during this period.

Who it helps

Readers looking for an introduction to AI history can use the section as a starting point. Within my portfolio, it demonstrates technical communication through a defined contribution to a shared presentation.
