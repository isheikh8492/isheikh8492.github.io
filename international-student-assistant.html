Group project

International Student Support Assistant

A group chatbot prototype exploring how conversational AI could support international-student questions.

Explore the work

The live application is named “DSO Daddy.” It identifies itself as an independent student project rather than an official university service. The project brief summarizes the documented work and distinguishes my contribution from the group’s platform-building work.

Overview

Our group chose an international-student support assistant because members were familiar with the questions students face around visas and studying in the United States. We explored a conversational interface for those questions, starting with student-related topics rather than expanding immediately into work visas.

What I built

The group created a Lovable-based chatbot prototype, documented in planning notes and available through a live web application. Its interface offers topic entry points for Visa & I-20, Admissions, CPT & OPT, and Student life, alongside a chat input.

My contribution

I contributed to ideation, choosing the problem, testing prompts, and planning the assistant.

The goal

Explore whether an AI assistant could make information about international-student questions easier to approach. The group planned an initial focus on student visa topics and proposed directing users to a designated school official or immigration lawyer when needed.

My approach

  1. Choose a problem with a clear audience. The group considered an international-student assistant and a fitness chatbot, then chose the student-support idea based on members’ familiarity with the problem.
  2. Set an initial scope. The planning notes prioritize student-related visa questions before broader work-visa topics.
  3. Explore initial prompting. The notes record ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot being used with the same initial question: “What documents are needed for travel on OPT?” They also record questions about entering the United States and transferring schools.
  4. Trial building platforms as a team. The notes describe one member exploring Lovable and another exploring a Chatbase trial. They report a positive initial response to the Lovable prototype, but do not include a structured platform evaluation.

The notes preserve initial questions and sample answers, but do not preserve separate outputs from all three platforms or a scored comparison. No comparative performance claim is made here.

Tools & technologies

  • ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot: recorded in the group’s initial prompting activity.
  • Lovable: used for the group’s chatbot prototype and the linked live application.
  • Chatbase: explored by another group member during the platform-trial stage.

Why it matters

What makes it useful

The project connects a familiar student problem to an initial assistant scope, prompt exploration, and a working conversational interface. The case study makes the early decisions visible alongside the prototype, showing how the group moved from a familiar problem to an initial assistant concept.

Who it helps

The intended users are international students with questions about studying at IWU and student-related visa topics. For an employer audience, the case study shows my involvement in problem selection, prompt testing, and assistant planning within a group project.

Prototype status

The public app loaded without sign-in and responded to a general scope-and-limits question during review. This confirms basic functionality, not immigration-answer accuracy, complete university-source coverage, or a tested retrieval architecture. The supplied notes do not document a structured accuracy evaluation or measured student outcomes.
